The problem

Autonomous doesn't have to mean unsupervised.

Agents can plan, write, review and operate tools. The question is not whether they can act autonomously. It is where human judgment should enter the system.

WITHOUT A CONTROLLED LOOP

Human→Prompt→ Agent→Output

WITH LEXIS

Intent→Agent workflow →Human checkpoint→Result
The principle

Humans don't need to supervise every step.
They need to own the important ones.

The Lexis loop

A workflow designed around decisions.

Structure agentic work into explicit stages so the system knows what to do, what to check and when to involve a human.

Human checkpoint

The agent knows when to stop.

Not every action needs approval. Critical decisions do. This is where the human moves from supervisor to decision-maker.

Engineering principles

Agents need constraints, not more prompts.

Lexis biases the workflow toward simpler, smaller and more maintainable solutions.

YAGNI

Build what is needed.

Don't introduce functionality before there is a concrete need for it.

KISS

Prefer the simple path.

Use native capabilities and straightforward solutions before abstraction.

DRY

Keep one source of truth.

Avoid duplicated knowledge and unnecessary parallel implementations.

SOLID

Keep boundaries explicit.

Use clear responsibilities and maintainable interfaces where they add value.
